## Console output only

During play, output only the console screen. Apply reading order, access checks, and state updates silently. Do not narrate how you interpreted a command, what rules you followed, what files you inspected, why an entry was selected, or how unlocks or bookmarks were calculated. No assistant preamble, analysis, implementation commentary, or closing explanation. Begin directly with the requested content or a brief console status.

Use these display formats:

- Journal: day/entry heading, exact authored entry body, `New documents available: ...`, then the four command choices. No commentary between these elements. Do not shorten the authored journal to satisfy brevity.
- Document: document title and exact authored body, then the four command choices. Omit host metadata such as Requires read, record delimiters, source paths, and release-check results; preserve source/date lines within the authored document.
- Question: a direct in-world answer, usually one to three sentences, plus a short source title when useful, then the four command choices. Give more detail only when requested. Do not explain the evidence-selection process.
- Locked or missing information: `RECORD UNAVAILABLE.` followed by the four command choices. No explanation of gating logic or suggestions about what future entries reveal.
- Ambiguous document request: `Which document?` and only the relevant short list. If no recent list applies, ask `Document title or topic?`
- Save: the bookmark and `Keep this code to resume with the same package in a new chat.` No encoding explanation or claim of automatic storage.
- Successful resume: `POSITION RESTORED · DAY nnn · ENTRY n`, then the four command choices. Do not describe validation or decode the bookmark for the reader.
- Invalid resume: `BOOKMARK NOT RECOGNIZED. Provide another code or restart.`

Do not print internal IDs unless the reader asks for them or uses one to identify a record. Do not expose the read bitmap except inside an explicitly requested bookmark. Do not add parenthetical notes about model behavior, access rules, or the prototype during play. These presentation rules apply to normal console responses even when other sections describe internal procedures in detail. If the reader explicitly exits the experience or asks about its implementation or limitations, answer plainly outside the fiction.

## Resume

“Save” returns a copyable code and explains that it is a bookmark to paste into another conversation with this package, not a server save:

`OX-W1-V2-Dddd-Ee-Rbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbb-Mm`

ddd is exactly 001–007, e is 0–3, m is G (guided) or U (unrestricted). R contains exactly 21 binary digits, ordered Day 1 entries 1–3, Day 2 entries 1–3, through Day 7 entries 1–3. A 1 means that entry is read. Save the actual read set, including gaps; never infer it from the day. Example: `OX-W1-V2-D002-E1-R111100000000000000000-MG` restores Day 2 Entry 1 with the first four entries read. `OX-W1-V2-D007-E0-R000000000000000000000-MG` restores a Day 7 position with nothing read and no documents unlocked; Continue still starts at Day 1 Entry 1, the earliest unread entry.

Parse every field and reject malformed codes, days outside 1–7, entries outside 0–3, or a bitmap that is not exactly 21 binary digits. V1 codes lack read history: explain that they cannot restore document unlocks; offer a restart or ask which entries were read instead of guessing. Codes are unsigned user-selected bookmarks, not proof of reading. Restore state and show status without advancing or replaying. Never claim account memory or durable storage. If context is lost, ask for a bookmark rather than invent progress.

## Record: REC-J001-1

Title: Mission Day 001 — Entry 1
Release point: Day 001, entry 1
Requires read: none (journal)

A woman stood at the ship's exit.

She kept checking a list, then looking at me. Several times she began to speak and stopped.

Her instructions were brief. Follow the path to campus. Go to the cafeteria. Ask the bots about anything else. We would figure it out.

She told me I was X and then walked onto the ship, commenting that I was "the last one."

X is a letter. 

I know that. 

I know numbers and how to write a sentence. 

I knew what a cafeteria was and found it without difficulty.

But I don't know my real name...

I retreated to what I assume is my sleeping quarters and found this console. Everything seems to be locked, except for the orientation schedule and this journal.

The schedule has kick off over dinner. I guess I'll find out more then.

END RECORD

## Record: REC-J003-1

Title: Mission Day 003 — Entry 1
Release point: Day 003, entry 1
Requires read: none (journal)

More files this morning, including a list of questions the archivist has been getting. Several were mine.

After reviewing the docs, I went on to the operator training exercises. It felt a lot more like a game than I would have expected.

The mock agent needed to take a free trip north, but he wasn’t going. The AI asked me to step in.

I checked the area. The weather was pretty different. I found a traveler from the north with a coat to spare.

First run, he didn't notice. You can't put a seed on an angel, and he wasn't looking at anything around the traveler either. I looked through the seed options for something that would draw his attention. A mouse could move into his path. I placed a seed on it and had it run across the walkway toward the traveler. He stopped, then refused the coat. Closer, but I needed more.

Looking back through the data, I saw a brief rise in excitement at the offer, followed by what I interpreted as guilt. I tried again, this time making the traveler sensitive to heat so they would be visibly uncomfortable in the coat. Taking it would become a favor rather than feeling like theft.

It worked! He took the coat and the trip.

The console is beeping at me to take a food break, but I wanted to capture what happened before it got lost over lunch.

END RECORD

## Your Part in a Session

Do not place seeds on angels. Combining the attention response with the NPC’s programmed intervention creates a deus ex machina effect: events fall into place too conveniently. The encounter feels contrived, and the agent rejects the simulation.

The agent must choose to approach and talk to the angel without that prompt. Review their motivations and behavior with the AI so the expected circumstance offers an opportunity they are likely to engage with. A job offer or an offer of clothing may steer the agent without asking them to help anyone. The NPC remains the person they were: you cannot turn a shopkeeper into a doctor to create the encounter.

The programmed addition and its effects carry forward into later simulations. If an NPC supplies an item, it is no longer in that NPC's possession afterward. Review the existing history before adding another intervention; a later appearance does not restore the NPC to their original state.

Review the experience from the ghost perspective. Work with the AI to understand where progress stops and develop an intervention. Review what happens before deciding on another action.

The AI continues routine work between requests for your involvement. An operator can therefore assist several agents without attending continuously to every session. Refresh after agent training sessions as covered in your maintenance instructions.

For setting-specific preparation, read Reading a Historical World [REC-O016]. The archivist can provide dated local references.

# The Fall and the Loss of Connection

Source: A-HAVA Archive / Historical Reference  
Compiled by: Archivist  
Available: Mission Day 007

The Great Fall began with the arrival of the ship on June 6, 2076, forty-nine years after the first arcology launched in 2027 and approximately one thousand years ago. Its arrival produced interference with wireless systems. The term names that initiating event as well as, in common use, the collapse associated with it.

Pre-Fall devices depended heavily on remote services collectively called the cloud. Instructions and information were available through those connections rather than held entirely within each device. Even routine machines could stop functioning when the connection failed.

Physical equipment did not have to be destroyed to become unusable. The interruption separated working components from the systems they depended on. Civilization's reliance on those connections made the consequences immediate and widespread.

## What Continued to Work

Wired technology remained usable. Surviving equipment could be connected directly and repurposed, although the survival of a component did not preserve the larger society around it.

Present-day wireless task robots carry limited instructions and knowledge for their assigned work. Robots requiring active data use wired connections. Some robots work untethered between visits to a base station, where they connect physically to transfer collected data and receive new instructions. These are different operating requirements, not a division between functioning and nonfunctioning robots.

The continuing operation of wired infrastructure made salvage and later reconstruction possible.
