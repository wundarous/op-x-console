## Resume

“Save” returns a copyable code and explains that it is a bookmark to paste into another conversation with this package, not a server save:

`OX-W1-V2-Dddd-Ee-Rbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbb-Mm`

ddd is exactly 001–007, e is 0–3, m is G (guided) or U (unrestricted). R contains exactly 21 binary digits, ordered Day 1 entries 1–3, Day 2 entries 1–3, through Day 7 entries 1–3. A 1 means that entry is read. Save the actual read set, including gaps; never infer it from the day. Example: `OX-W1-V2-D002-E1-R111100000000000000000-MG` restores Day 2 Entry 1 with the first four entries read. `OX-W1-V2-D007-E0-R000000000000000000000-MG` restores a Day 7 position with nothing read and no documents unlocked; Continue still starts at Day 1 Entry 1, the earliest unread entry.

Parse every field and reject malformed codes, days outside 1–7, entries outside 0–3, or a bitmap that is not exactly 21 binary digits. V1 codes lack read history: explain that they cannot restore document unlocks; offer a restart or ask which entries were read instead of guessing. Codes are unsigned user-selected bookmarks, not proof of reading. Restore state and show status without advancing or replaying. Never claim account memory or durable storage. If context is lost, ask for a bookmark rather than invent progress.

# Operator Console: First Use

Source: A-HAVA Orientation  
Release: Mission Day 002, after the console demonstration

A-HAVA prepares agents for ascension through AI-managed training. Most people progress without extensive human intervention. Operators assist when the AI can no longer move an agent forward.

Training work takes place through the console. You do not meet the agent in person during training.

Agents are identified by random anonymous tag names. A tag identifies an assignment without providing the person's real identity. It is separate from the access code used for a ghost-review session at the refresh pods.

## Working Terms

| Term | Use in the console |
| --- | --- |
| Agent | The person undertaking training. |
| Vessel | The character the agent inhabits within a training world. |
| Level | A repeatable scenario with a defined training goal. |
| Ghost | A fixed record of the agent's session, available for review. |
| Seed | Placed on an object or person in a level to draw the agent's attention through a brief sixth-sense response. |
| Angel | An NPC programmed with a function that nudges the agent in a direction while preserving the NPC’s character and purpose. |

## Your Part in a Session

Do not place seeds on angels. Combining the attention response with the NPC’s programmed intervention creates a deus ex machina effect: events fall into place too conveniently. The encounter feels contrived, and the agent rejects the simulation.

The agent must choose to approach and talk to the angel without that prompt. Review their motivations and behavior with the AI so the expected circumstance offers an opportunity they are likely to engage with. A job offer or an offer of clothing may steer the agent without asking them to help anyone. The NPC remains the person they were: you cannot turn a shopkeeper into a doctor to create the encounter.

The programmed addition and its effects carry forward into later simulations. If an NPC supplies an item, it is no longer in that NPC's possession afterward. Review the existing history before adding another intervention; a later appearance does not restore the NPC to their original state.

Review the experience from the ghost perspective. Work with the AI to understand where progress stops and develop an intervention. Review what happens before deciding on another action.

The AI continues routine work between requests for your involvement. An operator can therefore assist several agents without attending continuously to every session. Refresh after agent training sessions as covered in your maintenance instructions.

## Today's Exercise

Open the constructed training example provided in the demonstration. Locate its level goal, review the supplied ghost, and find the AI's request for assistance. Become familiar with where an intervention would be prepared and where its outcome would be reviewed.

This example is an exercise, not an assigned person. Today is for finding your way around the console. Guided intervention practice and readiness exercises follow on Day 003. Actual assignments begin on Day 004.

# The Fall and the Loss of Connection

Source: A-HAVA Archive / Historical Reference  
Compiled by: Archivist  
Available: Mission Day 007

The Great Fall began with the arrival of the ship, approximately one thousand years before the current Mission. Its arrival produced interference with wireless systems. The term names that initiating event as well as, in common use, the collapse associated with it.

Pre-Fall devices depended heavily on remote services collectively called the cloud. Instructions and information were available through those connections rather than held entirely within each device. Even routine machines could stop functioning when the connection failed.

Physical equipment did not have to be destroyed to become unusable. The interruption separated working components from the systems they depended on. Civilization's reliance on those connections made the consequences immediate and widespread.

## What Continued to Work

Wired technology remained usable. Surviving equipment could be connected directly and repurposed, although the survival of a component did not preserve the larger society around it.

Present-day wireless task robots carry limited instructions and knowledge for their assigned work. Robots requiring active data use wired connections. These are different operating requirements, not a division between functioning and nonfunctioning robots.

The continuing operation of wired infrastructure made salvage and later reconstruction possible.

# Life in the Monolithic Cities

Source: A-HAVA Archive / Historical Reference
